# Post-flood recovery clock (Task 7a)

Generated by `src/compute/post_flood.py` (via a one-off analysis script; the
numbers here can be reproduced by calling `smap_days_to_normal()`,
`water_persistence()` and `earliest_sowing_date()` with the arguments below).
Do not edit by hand.

This answers one question for a flooded plot: **when can it be sown again?**
Two independent NASA signals, combined:

- **SMAP root-zone soil moisture** says when the soil is dry enough. "Normal"
  for a given calendar day is the 80th percentile of that day (+/- 15 days)
  across every *other* year on record, held for 7 days in a row so a single
  dry reading right after a storm doesn't count as recovery. The 70th and
  90th percentiles are reported too, as a sensitivity check, not a second
  opinion to average in.
- **OPERA DSWx-S1** (Sentinel-1 radar, 30 m, weather- and cloud-independent)
  says when standing water was last seen in a 300 m x 300 m window around
  the point.

`earliest_sowing_date` is the **later** of the two. No extra buffer is added
beyond that (CLAUDE.md: no buffer unless it is sourced from
`data/reference/`).

**This is the flood -> late-sowing part of the cascade CLAUDE.md asks for.**
The remaining step -- re-reading the risk calendar (Task 6, `risk_calendar.py`)
at the new date to see how the risk changed -- is not built yet, so the
`crops` field below reports "research pending" for every crop rather than
guessing a sowing window: `data/reference/crop_calendar.csv` does not exist
yet (no source, no row, per CLAUDE.md).

### Caveat: DSWx-S1 at the Noakhali and Brahmanbaria points

At Feni and Cumilla, the 300 m window around the point never showed open
water/inundated vegetation (WTR band, Task 7a's `water_persistence()`) in
any of the 39-55 cloud-free-equivalent (radar, so really "layover/shadow
free") observations from 2024-08-21 to 2024-12-31 -- soil moisture alone
sets the earliest sowing date there.

At **Noakhali and Brahmanbaria**, `water_fraction` is small (0.01-0.18) but
present in nearly every observation through the end of the fetched window
(2024-12-31), without the declining trend a draining flood would show. The
straightforward reading is a small permanent or near-permanent water
feature (a pond, khal or river edge) inside the 300 m window at these two
points, not floodwater still standing four months later. Taking
`water_persistence()`'s output literally would push Noakhali's earliest
sowing date to 2025-01-01 and flag Brahmanbaria's DSWx-S1 date as "still not
gone" as late as 2024-12-31 even though its SMAP-based date (2024-09-17) is
much earlier -- both are almost certainly wrong as a *flood-recovery* signal.
**For these two districts this demo reports the SMAP-only date
(earliest_sowing_date driven by soil moisture) and flags DSWx-S1 as
inconclusive here**, rather than silently combining a spurious "still
flooded" reading into the headline number. This is exactly the kind of thing
CLAUDE.md's "NASA data for the area around your field, not your field" line
is warning about: the district point is a representative coordinate, not a
verified location inside the flooded plot, and DSWx-S1's 300 m window can
land on a feature (a pond) that has nothing to do with the field a farmer is
asking about. A real deployment would let the user drop a pin on their own
plot (Task 7b) instead of using the district point.

### Sensitivity note (70th / 80th / 90th percentile)

The 90th-percentile threshold (easier to satisfy: "just a bit wetter than
usual is fine") recovers no later than the 80th at every district, as
expected. The 70th-percentile threshold (stricter) is usually a little
slower, except Brahmanbaria, where it jumps to 55 days versus 27 for the
80th -- the real trajectory hovers close to more than one of these
thresholds in that window, so a short dip briefly satisfies the looser
threshold's hold-7-days rule before the stricter one's. This is a reason to
treat the headline number as "recovered enough by a reasonable definition
of normal," not a precise day, and to always show the sensitivity range
alongside it (which CLAUDE.md's honesty rules ask for).

## Coverage note: why DSWx-S1 was only fetched through 2024-12-31

`scripts/fetch_dswx.py` timed 5 real granule reads (windowed HTTPS range
requests via `earthaccess` + `rasterio`, no full-scene download) at
**~4.1 s/granule**, then a fuller sample at **~3.4-3.5 s/granule** once
warmed up. CMR has:

| District | Granules, 2024-08-21 to today (2026-09-27) | Granules, 2024-08-21 to 2024-12-31 |
|---|---|---|
| Feni | 276 | 54 |
| Cumilla | 568 | 110 |
| Noakhali | 480 | 95 |
| Brahmanbaria | 292 | 56 |
| **Total** | **1,616** | **315** |

Full history: ~1,616 granules x ~3.5 s = **~94 minutes**. This run fetched
just the 315 granules through 2024-12-31 (**~18 minutes** actual) -- enough
to cover the flood, the SMAP recovery window, and a full rabi sowing season
after it, for this demo. `fetch_dswx.py` is resumable (already-saved granule
IDs are skipped), so extending any district's file to the present is
`python scripts/fetch_dswx.py <district>` and a ~1 hour wait, not a rewrite.

## Data used

- SMAP root-zone soil moisture: `data/processed/smap_<district>_2015_2026.csv`
  (already in the repo from an earlier task).
- OPERA DSWx-S1: `data/processed/dswx_<district>.csv`, built by
  `scripts/fetch_dswx.py` for this task (WTR band; codes 0/1/3 = not
  water/open water/inundated vegetation are "valid", 250/251/255 =
  HAND-masked/layover-shadow-masked/fill are excluded from both the water
  and the valid-observation counts).
